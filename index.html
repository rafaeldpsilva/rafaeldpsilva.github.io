Learning occupant preferences and resolving comfort conflicts with heterogeneous graph neural networks

Rafael Silva · Doctoral research · {{ today }}
A scrolling account of each development phase: what was built, how, and why.

A building should keep every occupant comfortable with the fewest manual interventions, and resolve disagreements in shared rooms fairly and explainably. We model each moment of a building as a heterogeneous graph of people, rooms, devices and sensors. One head learns each person's preferred setpoint with its uncertainty; a second weighs the people sharing a room to choose one setpoint. A simulator with hidden ground truth makes the recovery of preferences measurable before the same pipeline runs on real buildings.

§1Problem and goal

Comfort is personal, rooms are shared, and people rarely say what they want — they press a button when something is wrong.

The problem

Thermostats treat a room as if it had one occupant with a fixed preference. Real rooms hold several people whose preferences differ, change with activity, and are expressed only indirectly, through overrides. Many presses are anonymous: a shared remote does not say who pressed it.

The goal

Keep every occupant comfortable with the fewest manual interventions, resolve shared-space conflicts fairly, and explain every decision. Version 1 is the simplest model that works and is frozen as a baseline; each extension is one paper compared against it.
